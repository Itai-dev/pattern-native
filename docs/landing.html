Pain feels random because memory is the wrong instrument.

Memory keeps the worst day and the latest one and loses the rest. It cannot tell you whether this month is worse than last, and it cannot explain the last three months to anyone in twelve minutes. A record can. Pattern is that record, with three things it does for you.

Understand

See your own days.

Ninety squares. Which end of your own range a day sat in. Where it hurt, the words you used, whether mornings really are worse. Your days shown back without memory's editing.

Manage

Test what you suspect.

Name one thing. Pattern asks about it the same way every day, alongside one thing you didn't suspect, so the answer is checkable rather than a hunch. Sleep and movement arrive from Apple Health without being asked for.

Explain

Hand over one page.

The same record becomes a summary a clinician reads in thirty seconds, in the order they ask, with your own question at the top. The appointment stops being an improvisation.

The check-in

One number. That's the whole check-in.

How intense is your pain right now, zero to ten. Move the slider, tap Log it, done. Pain is the only mandatory answer and a pain-only entry is never called incomplete, anywhere.

Where it hurts, how it feels and what moved it are offered, never demanded, asked at most once a day, and never more than three screens deep. A morning question is asked in the morning. An evening question waits for the evening. A question outside its window isn't asked at all, because an answer given at the wrong time is worse than a gap.

Nothing rewards opening the app. No streaks, no rings, no percentages. The record changes only when you add to it.

Apple Watch

From your wrist, without your phone.

A complication on the face opens the check-in. Turn the crown, or tap the square's halves if turning hurts your hands. When the value has been still for a beat, a check appears. Tap it, and you have three seconds to take it back.

The watch records the number only. Everything else is what the phone is for, and a 40mm screen is the strongest argument that principle will ever get.

The watch never writes the record. It hands the number to your iPhone, which writes it through the same path as every other check-in, the next time Pattern opens. The watch says so, on its own screen.

Apple Health

Sleep and movement, beside the number.

Connect the categories you choose. Last night's sleep, yesterday's steps, a workout, time upright: each sits on the day it belongs to, next to the pain you recorded. Read-only. Never written back. Never sent anywhere.

A comparison is drawn only when it is lawful: mornings against the night before, evenings against the hours before the check-in, split at your own lowest and highest third. It needs fourteen paired days, five in each group, and a point and a half of difference before it says anything. Then it says "possible", with the numbers, and the sentence that it is not proof of cause.

If a pattern stops holding as more days arrive, Pattern says that too. Watching a pattern fade is as informative as watching one appear.

The point

A pain tracker that refuses to tell you what's causing your pain.

Every item below would raise engagement. Each is refused, and the refusals are the product.

  • No streaks, rings or badges.A person in a flare who feels judged deletes the app. A missed day is just a missed day.
  • No invented scores.The number is what you entered. No composite, no "wellness index", no rolling average that improves by the calendar.
  • No causes before the evidence.Nothing is called a trigger. An association shows with its sample size or not at all, and never as a list.
  • No week-over-week.No arrows, no "down 12%". Nothing on any screen differs between two opens of the same day except what you added.
  • No medication advice. Ever.Medication is recorded because leaving it out would be dishonest. It is never analysed.
  • No account, no upload.Nothing you tell it about your body leaves the phone. There is no server that could receive it.
  • No colour without meaning.A hue on any screen carries a pain score. Buttons are white. Counts are white. Nothing is tinted for decoration.
  • No AI.Every number you see comes from deterministic code with its thresholds in one file, argued in comments you can read.

The clinician page

One page. Thirty seconds. In the order they ask.

Severity, site, timing, character, what makes it better or worse, what was tried and how it went. Your background and your own question at the top, in your words. Sample sizes beside every figure. Gaps left as gaps.

Made on the phone, handed to the share sheet, sent only where you send it. It says on its face that it is self-recorded and not a diagnosis.

Two days before an appointment you've told it about, Pattern offers the page on the home screen. That is the one thing it will ever bring up on its own.

Under the hood

For people who read the spec.

StorageLocal SQLite on the phone. No account, no login, no sync, no server. Deleting the app deletes the record.
Data modelEvery optional answer has three states that survive storage, backup and migration: never asked, asked and skipped, answered. A skip is never read as a negative.
TimestampsLocal minute, UTC instant and offset at capture. Never backfilled. A check-in recalled later is marked as such forever.
ThresholdsOne file, named constants, each with the arithmetic that argues for it. Eight observations per group, 1.5 points, direction stable across both halves of the record.
EngineDeterministic and local. Outer terciles of your own distribution, middle third discarded. Four verdicts: insufficient, observation, possible, fading. Runs dark on the focus questions until a noise harness passes.
Apple HealthRead-only, per-category consent through Apple's own sheet. Sleep merged to its asleep-union and filed under the morning it ends on. Steps from the one source that saw the most, never summed across devices. Excluded from backups.
Apple WatchWatchConnectivity user-info transfers, queued through reboots. The phone is the only writer. The complication shows no data.
WidgetsLock screen (circular, rectangular, inline) and home screen (small, medium, large). Only colours and the number you entered. Discreet on the lock screen unless you turn the number on. Rolls over at midnight.
BackupJSON export and restore, validated fully before anything is touched, with replace or merge. Row ids are not trusted across devices; merges deduplicate on content.
AnalyticsCounts only, to Aptabase in the EU, under a random id linked to nothing: that a check-in happened and how long it took, never what it said. Event names from a closed list, values capped at 24 characters. Off in Profile.
StackExpo SDK 57, React Native, TypeScript. SwiftUI for the watch and the complication. Updates over the air; native targets through TestFlight.
